UFO Fishing and UFO Parasail

One brand designed for two different adventures

UFO Fishing and UFO Parasail share an ocean-focused brand, but their customers need help making different decisions.

I designed the websites as sister experiences: consistent enough to feel connected, but distinct enough to support separate audiences, booking journeys, and search strategies.

The challenge

The websites needed to feel like part of the same brand without becoming copies of one another.

Fishing visitors needed help choosing a charter. Parasailing visitors needed to select an island, compare flights, and feel confident about safety before booking.

The approach

I created a shared foundation for color, typography, photography, components, and booking behavior, then adapted each site around its primary customer decision.

The outcome

Two connected experiences with a clear purpose:

  • UFO Fishing: Find the right fishing charter.
  • UFO Parasail: Choose an island and flight option.

Shared system, distinct experiences

Both websites use:

  • Ocean-inspired color
  • Friendly, modern typography
  • Authentic guest photography
  • Rounded comparison cards
  • Clear trust content
  • High-contrast booking actions
  • Mobile-first information hierarchy

UFO Fishing feels deeper and more exploratory, using imagery of catches, crew members, families, and life on the water.

UFO Parasail feels lighter and more open, using sky, coastline, horizon, and in-flight imagery to communicate the feeling of seeing Hawaii from above.

UFO Fishing

The fishing homepage was designed around one customer question:

Which trip is right for me?

Guided pathways, charter comparisons, and a seasonal What’s Biting feature helped visitors choose based on experience level, group type, trip length, and fishing goals.

UFO Parasail

The parasailing homepage began with a simpler decision:

Maui or Kona?

After choosing an island, visitors could compare the 800-foot and 1,200-foot flights, review participation requirements, understand the boarding process, and check availability.

Safety information, reviews, and answers for first-time flyers appeared near the booking decision rather than being buried farther down the page.

Key design decisions

01

Design around the customer’s first decision

Fishing starts with trip selection. Parasailing starts with location. Each website’s structure reflects that difference.

02

Keep booking information early

Mobile visitors began dropping off around 20–30% scroll depth, so comparison details, trust signals, and booking actions were placed near the top.

03

Connect the brands without combining them

The websites share recognizable visual and interaction patterns while maintaining separate domains, content, and search targets.

Reflection

The goal was not to make two identical websites. It was to create a shared design language that could flex around two different customer journeys.

The result gives UFO a more consistent digital brand while making each adventure easier to understand and book.
