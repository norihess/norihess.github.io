Sea Maui

Designing the experience around the people who make it memorable

Role
Sr. Web Designer
Timeline
3 weeks · Discovery through developer handoff
Scope
Discovery, experience strategy, responsive web design, component system, and developer documentation
Overview What shaped the design Key decisions Visual direction Clarifying the experience Responsive system Developer handoff Reflection
The challenge

Modernize a website that already converted, and show what makes Sea Maui different.

The approach

Keep the proven booking path. Put guests and crew at the center of the story.

The outcome

A responsive, people-first site with reusable components and a seasonal content system.

What shaped the design

I led discovery with Sea Maui’s stakeholders, which revealed four priorities:

  • Families and couples were the primary audiences.
  • Public tours needed greater emphasis than private charters.
  • FAQs and What’s Included were the most-used activity-page content.
  • Guests needed clearer expectations about beach loading, destinations, and the family-friendly experience.

I used these findings to prioritize clarity and confidence over unnecessary restructuring.

Key design decisions

01

Show people, not just boats

I built the visual story around real guest moments, crew interactions, families, couples, and life on the water. Boats and Maui scenery support the story rather than becoming the entire story.

Responsive homepage. Scroll inside each frame.
02

Preserve the path to booking

The existing booking structure was already working, so I kept prominent calls to action and featured activities familiar while making the surrounding content and visual design more distinctive.

03

Make activity discovery responsive

I designed featured activities as a more visual browsing experience on desktop and a simpler stacked presentation on mobile, making tours easy to compare across screen sizes.

04

Design seasonality into the system

I treated whale watching as a reusable seasonal state with promotional, seasonal, and out-of-season treatments instead of a page that had to be repeatedly added and removed.

Visual direction

I combined large experiential photography with organic shapes, wavy transitions, rounded components, playful color blocking, and generous spacing.

The result feels recognizably Sea Maui: polished but not pretentious, energetic but not overwhelming, and premium without becoming a luxury-resort experience.

Why Montserrat and Karla

Montserrat’s wide, geometric letterforms give headlines a confident, friendly voice that holds up over full-bleed ocean photography. Karla pairs with it as a relaxed, slightly quirky grotesque that stays legible at small sizes on mobile, so planning details like times, inclusions and FAQs read easily without feeling corporate.

Clarifying the experience

I used photography, video, FAQs, and destination content to answer questions before booking.

I designed a Where Do We Go experience around the destinations Sea Maui visits, and used visual storytelling to make beach loading easier to understand than text alone.

Responsive system

I created reusable patterns for:

The system gave the website a consistent visual identity while keeping content flexible and maintainable.

  • Activity cards
  • Booking actions
  • Seasonal badges and states
  • FAQ and What’s Included content
  • Image and video galleries
  • Crew storytelling
  • Destination information
  • Desktop and mobile navigation

Developer handoff

Over three weeks, I led the project from stakeholder discovery to responsive design and developer-ready documentation.

My handoff defined component behavior, responsive layouts, visual styles, content priorities, and seasonal states so the experience could be implemented consistently.

Reflection

The strongest solution I found was not a complete reinvention. It was a focused evolution that protected proven booking behavior while making Sea Maui’s most valuable differentiator—its people—visible throughout the experience.
